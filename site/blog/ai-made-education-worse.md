---
title: Education was bad; generative AI made it worse
---

Generative AI can be an awesome tool for writing. It can be great to brainstorm, to get a certain sentence just right or checking your text doesn't contain mistakes. But... that is not what most students at my school use it for.

And that's a problem. School *should* be, among other things, about teaching people important knowledge and skills, and maybe even more importantly, it should get people excited about learning. But if students just use generative AI to do their assignments, school clearly fails at that. And I completely understand that schools need to do something "against" using generative AI. But schools can't seem to do this right.

My school has tried multiple 'solutions' for this problem, such as taking away your ability to use generative AI (for example by forcing us to use some weird ancient sheet of cellulose fibres called paper) or making it so that generative AI cannot really help students with something, for example by forcing them to build something (i.e. a poster for example).

What schools would ideally want is that their students don't *want* to use generative AI. The experience of learning new things should be engaging and exciting. But that is not the case, and it wasn't the case before generative AI played such a big role.

And the students aren't the problem: the endless obsession with assignments, tests, grades and results is. The endless amount of boring, tedious, and repetitive assignments makes people forget that school can be fun. So for almost everyone, learning is not an interesting experience: it's a chore, so it's no wonder they would rather let a computer do it.
